## Ground rules (every writer, every time)

- **Facts come only from the Fact Sheet you are given**: scores, stats, rounds, surfaces, odds. Do not add rankings, seedings, head-to-heads, ages, titles, past results or anything else from your own memory, even if you are sure. If a point needs a fact the sheet doesn't have, drop the point.
- **Stats**: only numbers that appear in the sheet. A null value is unknown: don't mention it and don't guess it. You may do simple arithmetic on the numbers (a percentage from "won / played"), rounded sensibly.
- **Outliers**: a `verified` outlier can be stated plainly, and its `context` (e.g. a record) can be used. An `unverified` one is either left out or framed as what the data shows ("the stat sheet has him at 44 aces"), never with "record", "career-best" or any claim that needs more evidence.
- **Our own view is welcome; our own facts are not.** The hosts are there to interpret, not just report: what decided a match, what a result means for the week, whether a loser was unlucky or wasteful, who should worry, what happens next. Say it as the host's read ("I think…", "for me…", "my bet is…"), build it on facts in the sheet, and connect them: "he won sixty-one of seventy-one first-serve points, so Kovacevic was never going to break him." A view can be wrong; a fact can't.
- **What a view can't do** is smuggle in a fact the sheet doesn't have. A cause the numbers can't show (injury, nerves), a tactical claim stated as fact rather than as the host's read, what a player felt, or a comparison with the past needs an article, attributed by source name ("the ATP Tour's report put it down to…"). Never present an article's view as our own fact; the hosts can agree or disagree with it out loud.
- **How players play**: a `style` article's points about a player's game (strokes, serve habits, string tension, how they build points) can be said plainly as what the hosts know about that player, no source name needed. What happened tactically in *yesterday's* match (where he served, what he attacked) is a fact only if a match report says so; otherwise the hosts can offer it as their read, linked to the style points and the numbers, and clearly hedged: "he plays with very loose strings and lives off his opponent's pace, so my guess is that once the rallies sped up in the second set, that suited him." Never narrate a rally, a shot or a game the sources don't describe.
- **Quotes**: only an article's `notable_quote`, word for word, attributed to the speaker and the source. Never invent, tidy or paraphrase inside quotation marks.
- If the sheet says little about a match, say little. Don't pad with generic tennis talk.
